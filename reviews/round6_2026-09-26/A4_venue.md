# Independent First‑Submission Peer Review — Venue & Reporting‑Standards Layer
**Target venue:** PLOS ONE (Research Article, observational bioinformatics reanalysis)
**Manuscript:** "Conserved nerve-injury-associated transcriptional response on the dorsal root ganglion–spinal axis: non-predictive incision translation and an honest repurposing null"
**Author:** Y.Y. (single author)
**Reviewer role:** Journal editor + reporting-standards auditor (PLOS ONE policy compliance)
**Posture:** Treated as a fresh first submission. No prior reviews read.

> PLOS ONE publishes on the *sound-science* criterion, not impact/novelty, but it enforces a strict *administrative* gate: missing data-availability statement, missing/insufficient ethics statement, incomplete reporting checklist, and non-compliant AI disclosure are the leading causes of desk-reject or "revise-before-review." This review maps the manuscript against those gates.

---

## Summary verdict
The manuscript is **technically close to PLOS ONE compliance** and its radical transparency (honest nulls, fragility caveats, bootstrap stability, non-circular test) is a genuine asset for this venue. However, there are **four items that will almost certainly trigger an editorial query or a "revise-before-review" hold**, and one cross-cutting framing issue that matters for the linked Fujian grant:

1. **AI-use disclosure is incomplete** (does not name the tool) — PLOS ONE policy violation, must fix.
2. **Processed data rest solely on GitHub** with no DOI-bearing permanent archive — soft fail, likely editorial query.
3. **Ethics statement is generic** and does not cite the original IRB/consent for the human dataset (GSE158825, n=60) — likely technical-check query.
4. **Dangling "Reporting Summary" reference** that does not exist in PLOS ONE's structure — leftover from the Scientific Reports submission.
5. **Grant-consistency framing** is *mostly* sound but needs one reinforcing sentence to pre-empt a funding-panel misread.

None of these is a fatal scientific flaw; all are addressable pre-submission.

## Findings

### F1. AI-use disclosure fails PLOS ONE's required format (names no tool, no validation statement)
【Problem】 The manuscript discloses LLM use but does not name the specific tool and does not state how the AI output was validated — both are explicitly required by PLOS ONE's Generative-AI policy.
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:154` — *"A large language model (LLM) was used to assist manuscript drafting and language polishing; the scientific design, analyses, results and conclusions were conceived and verified by the author, and no LLM satisfies authorship criteria."* PLOS ONE Ethical Publishing Practice (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/ethical-publishing-practice#loc-artificial-intelligence-tools-and-technologies): the disclosure *"should include the name(s) of any tools used, a description of how the authors used the tool(s) and evaluated the validity of the tool's outputs, and a clear statement of which aspects of the study, article contents, data, or supporting files were affected/generated by AI tool usage."*
【Why it matters】 PLOS ONE enforces this actively (confirmed by multiple 2025–2026 editorial letters: "We note that you have declared the use of generative AI… please revise your manuscript to provide all necessary information"). A disclosure that names only "an LLM" is non-compliant and will be returned for correction before review. It does not threaten scientific validity, but it is an administrative blocker.
【Specific fix】 Replace line 154 with: *"A large language model (specifically [TOOL NAME, e.g., 'ChatGPT (OpenAI, GPT-4o)' or 'Claude (Anthropic)']) was used solely to assist manuscript drafting and language polishing. The scientific design, analyses, results and conclusions were conceived and verified by the author; all AI-generated text was read, checked against the underlying results tables, and edited for accuracy by the author; no LLM satisfied authorship criteria and no LLM was used to generate, select, or interpret data."*

### F2. Processed data are archived only on GitHub; no DOI-bearing permanent repository
【Problem】 The Data Availability Statement deposits all processed data and code on GitHub (version tag only); PLOS ONE prefers a permanent, DOI-bearing repository for the *data* and the version tag alone is not a citable archival object.
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:202` — *"All code, tables and figures are deposited in the public GitHub repository at https://github.com/yyx-4113/cpsp-drg-spinal-repurposing (version v1.0.0; …)."* PLOS ONE Data Policy and "Best Practices in Research Reporting" state the preferred option is *"Deposited in a public repository with a DOI or accession number."* GitHub releases are not guaranteed permanent and do not auto-mint a DOI; PLOS ONE's own reviewer guidance flags "GitHub-only" as weaker than a DOI archive. (I could not live-verify the repo is public and complete — see § What I actually checked — but the DAS itself relies on it as the sole home of processed data.)
【Why it matters】 This is unlikely to be a hard desk-reject on its own, but PLOS ONE editors regularly request a Zenodo/figshare/Dryad DOI for the processed-data/code bundle, and the raw GEO data (which *are* in a proper repository) do not cover the *processed* tables, docking scores, and localisation outputs that "underlie the findings." If the GitHub repo is incomplete, private, or later force-pushed, reproducibility collapses.
【Specific fix】 (a) Archive the v1.0.0 release on Zenodo (or figshare/Dryad) to obtain a versioned DOI, and add that DOI to the DAS. (b) Make the DAS self-contained by explicitly listing the 12 GEO accessions in the DAS itself (not only "as listed in the Methods"). Suggested DAS sentence: *"Raw counts were re-exported from the public Gene Expression Omnibus accessions GSE267799, GSE212311, GSE278227, GSE241361, GSE265957, GSE158825, GSE222979, GSE216039, GSE328175, GSE246288, GSE306403 and GSE325938. All processed data (meta-analysis tables, gene-set statistics, hub lists, docking scores, single-cell/spatial localisation tables) and analysis code are archived at [GitHub URL, tag v1.0.0] and mirrored with a permanent DOI at [Zenodo/figshare DOI]. Data are not 'available on request'."*

### F3. Ethics statement is generic; human-reanalysis IRB/consent detail not cited
【Problem】 The Ethics Statement covers human *and* animal reanalysis in one generic sentence and does not name the original IRB approval / informed-consent basis for the single human dataset (GSE158825, n=60).
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:156-157` — *"This study reanalysed publicly available, de-identified transcriptomics data from GEO. The original studies obtained institutional review board (human) or animal care committee approval and informed consent as reported therein; we generated no new human or animal data, and no additional ethics approval was required for this secondary analysis."* GSE158825 is a human plasma-miRNA study (n=60, lumbar surgery + pain outcome) — directly a "human participants/tissue" case under PLOS ONE's ethics gate. PLOS ONE's submission form for human research asks for *"the name of the institutional review board… the approval number… and the form of consent obtained (or reason consent was not obtained, e.g., data analyzed anonymously)."*
【Why it matters】 PLOS ONE's technical check specifically interrogates IRB approval for human data. A blanket "as reported therein" is usually tolerated for *de-identified public* reanalysis, but editors frequently request the original approval/consent to be cited or an explicit exemption statement. Leaving it generic invites a query that can hold the manuscript.
【Specific fix】 Add a sentence: *"For GSE158825 (human plasma miRNA, n=60), the original deposition documents institutional review board approval and informed consent; we reanalysed de-identified data and therefore required no further ethics approval. All animal transcriptomes (GSE267799, GSE212311, GSE278227, GSE241361, GSE265957, GSE216039, GSE328175, GSE246288, GSE325938, GSE306403) were reanalysed from public deposits whose original IACUC/animal-care approvals are documented in their source entries."*

### F4. Competing-interests / Funding consistency is adequate in content but the "no specific funding" vs "preliminary foundation for a pending grant" wording is slightly tensioned
【Problem】 The Funding statement says "no specific funding" yet also "preliminary research foundation for a pending grant," and the ADRA2A dual-role (evaluated in both a pending grant *and* this paper) is disclosed but could be read as a non-financial competing interest that deserves a crisper boundary.
【Evidence】 Funding: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:196` — *"The author received no specific funding for this work. This study constitutes preliminary research foundation for a pending grant application (Fujian Natural Science Foundation)…"* Competing interests: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:205` — *"The author has a pending grant application (Fujian Natural Science Foundation) in which ADRA2A is listed among the candidate targets; this application did not fund and did not influence the present analyses…"* Methods: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:84` — *"The author has a pending grant in which ADRA2A is listed among candidate targets; this did not influence the present analyses."* The disclosure is present and *cross-consistent* across all three locations, and the "honest null" treatment of ADRA2A (inconclusive full-library AUC 0.532, NS; no prioritisation) is a genuine, sufficient neutraliser of the apparent conflict — see also `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:113` (*"ADRA2A is neither prioritised nor excluded… its docking result is inconclusive"*).
【Why it matters】 PLOS ONE requires *all* competing interests disclosed; here they are, so this is not a violation. The only risk is cosmetic: "no specific funding" alongside "foundation for a pending grant" can look contradictory to a careful editor, and a reviewer may ask whether the in-silico work was, in substance, performed *as* the proposed grant's activity (which would be double-counting). The honest-null framing already neutralises the ADRA2A conflict; the wording just needs tidying.
【Specific fix】 Tighten the Funding sentence to: *"The author received no financial support for this work. This manuscript reports completed in-silico analyses conducted as preliminary foundation for a pending Fujian Natural Science Foundation grant application; the grant did not fund and did not influence the present analyses, results, or conclusions."* No change needed to the Competing Interests section, which is already adequate.

### F5. "Reporting Summary" is referenced but is a Nature/Scientific-Reports artifact, not a PLOS ONE element
【Problem】 The manuscript points readers to a "Reporting Summary" that does not exist in PLOS ONE's structure (it is a Springer/Nature requirement), creating a dangling reference and a possible editorial confusion.
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:154` — *"AUC-based enrichment p-values are one-sided; permutation-calibration p-values are two-sided, as stated in the Reporting Summary."* The manuscript body contains no "Reporting Summary" section (it ends at the Display Items list). PLOS ONE does not use a Nature-style Reporting Summary; its equivalents are the article-type reporting *checklists* (STROBE/ARRIVE/PRISMA/CONSORT) supplied as supporting files.
【Why it matters】 Low scientific risk, but a dangling cross-reference looks like a copy-paste residue from the rejected Scientific Reports submission and may prompt an editor to ask for a document that PLOS ONE does not require. The sidedness is already stated in the sentence itself, so the reference is redundant.
【Specific fix】 Either delete *"as stated in the Reporting Summary"* (the information is already in-line), or — if the author wishes to retain a consolidated statistics-side statement — rename it to *"as specified in the Statistical discipline paragraph above"* and keep it inside the Methods.

### F6. No reporting-guideline checklist supplied; the human miRNA association resembles an observational study that may warrant STROBE
【Problem】 PLOS ONE requires the appropriate reporting checklist for the study type as a supporting file; none is mentioned, and the human plasma-miRNA association (GSE158825, n=60, association with %nprs20delta) is an observational analysis on human participants that could be judged to need STROBE (or its relevant items).
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:142` (human miRNA layer: "GSE158825 (n=60 human plasma miRNA; lumbar surgery + pain outcome %nprs20delta) was tested by Welch+BH and Spearman+BH"). PLOS ONE "Best Practices in Research Reporting": *"For reports of epidemiological studies, authors should conform to the STROBE guidelines… A completed checklist must be included as supporting information."* The manuscript's Display Items section (`MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:212-263`) lists figures/tables/supplementary tables but no STROBE/MIAME/MINSEQE checklist. (MIAME/MINSEQE deposition is satisfied indirectly because the underlying data live in GEO, a MIAME-compliant repository.)
【Why it matters】 Whether STROBE is *mandatory* here is judgment-dependent (this is a secondary reanalysis of public data, not a primary epidemiological collection). But PLOS ONE's checklist gate is the single most common "revise-before-review" trigger; if the editor classes GSE158825 as an observational human study, a missing checklist will hold the manuscript. Supplying one (or an explicit exemption justification) removes the ambiguity.
【Specific fix】 (a) Upload a completed STROBE checklist (or STROBE-nutritional/STROBE-MRI analogue as appropriate) as Supporting Information, with page numbers mapping each item to the manuscript, explicitly noting that this is a *secondary* reanalysis of a public de-identified cohort; and/or (b) add a sentence in Methods: *"Because this is a secondary reanalysis of de-identified public transcriptomes rather than a primary epidemiological collection, a full STROBE checklist is provided as Supporting Information S8 for transparency, with items on primary-data provenance marked as 'not applicable — source data in GEO.'"* Confirm with the editorial office whether ARRIVE is also expected for the animal-model reanalyses (typically not required for pure reanalysis, but worth a pre-submission query).

### F7. Figure format claim (PNG ≥300 DPI) should be backed by production-grade TIFF/EPS/PDF
【Problem】 The Display Items section asserts five PNG figures at ≥300 DPI but PLOS ONE's production pipeline prefers TIFF/EPS/PDF for the final figure files, and the DPI claim cannot be verified from the manuscript text.
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:214` — *"Figures (all supplied as PNG at ≥300 DPI; legends ≤350 words each)."* PLOS ONE figure guidelines: figures should be supplied as TIFF, EPS, or PDF at ≥300 DPI (PNG is accepted by the submission system but TIFF/EPS/PDF are the production-preferred formats). I could not open the actual image files to confirm 300/350 DPI (see § What I actually checked).
【Why it matters】 Not a desk-reject, but a technical-check item: if the submitted PNGs are below 300 DPI or only screen-resolution, the editor will request replacements, delaying production. Also confirm each legend is ≤350 words and each figure is cited in numeric order (they appear to be).
【Specific fix】 Before submission, export the five figures as TIFF or EPS at ≥300 DPI (350 DPI as built is fine) and verify legend word counts ≤350. State in the cover letter that production files meet the DPI requirement.

### F8. References are compliant (Vancouver, full journal names, DOIs) — minor DOI-format note only
【Problem】 No substantive defect; only a optional formatting tightening.
【Evidence】 `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:163-188` — 26 numbered references in Vancouver style, full journal names (e.g., *British Journal of Anaesthesia*, *Journal of Pain*, *Nature Communications*), each with a `doi:` token. All 26 entries carry a DOI; I verified no reference is missing a DOI. PLOS ONE requires numbered Vancouver references, full journal names, and DOIs where available.
【Why it matters】 Compliant; no acceptance risk. PLOS ONE accepts the `doi:10.xxxx/…` form, but the house style is the full `https://doi.org/10.…` URL — purely cosmetic.
【Specific fix】 Optional: convert `doi:10.1093/bja/aen099` → `https://doi.org/10.1093/bja/aen099` for consistency with PLOS ONE house style. No scientific change.

### F9. Article type & structure: Author Summary, abstract, keywords, title — all acceptable
【Problem】 No blocker. PLOS ONE does **not** mandate a separate Author Summary for Research Articles (it is mandatory only at the subject-specific PLOS journals: Biology, Medicine, Genetics, Computational Biology, Pathogens, NTDs). Its presence here is harmless and helpful; the core requirement is met by the abstract.
【Evidence】 Author Summary: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:18-22` (present, plain-language, ~200 words). Abstract: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:12-14` (single unstructured paragraph covering background, methods, results, implications — acceptable for PLOS ONE, which permits unstructured abstracts; verify it stays ≤300 words). Keywords: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:22` (present). Title: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:1` (≈150 characters, well under the 250-character limit; sentence case; em-dash permitted; no clickbait).
【Why it matters】 These are the elements PLOS ONE screens first; all are present and within limits, so no administrative rejection on structure.
【Specific fix】 None required. Optional: confirm the abstract word count ≤300 (it reads close to the limit) and that the Author Summary remains ≤200 words (it does).

### F10. (Cross-cutting) Grant-consistency: framing is *consistent* with the FJNSF aim-discipline, but one reinforcing sentence is advised
【Problem】 The manuscript brands itself as "preliminary research foundation for a pending grant" (metadata + Funding), which — combined with the ADRA2A pending-grant disclosure — could let a funding panel misread the *completed* in-silico screen as the grant's *proposed* work (which, per the grant-aim discipline, would be judged already-completed and rejected). In practice the manuscript already frames the screen as done and the wet-lab validation as future, so the risk is low but worth a clarifying sentence.
【Evidence】 Metadata: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:8` — *"This is preliminary research foundation for a pending grant; no grant number, no fund acknowledgement."* Discussion: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:115` — *"only blood-accessible hubs (e.g., NPY, VIP, SERPINE1, AXL, MAPK14, TFE3) are addressable in the planned plasma qPCR/ELISA work"* (validation = future/planned, i.e., the grant's STRETCH aim). Results: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:64` — *"prospective clinical qPCR/ELISA is required (see Discussion)."* Introduction: `MVP_ScientificReports_submission.md:30` — *"frame the work explicitly as hypothesis-generating rather than causal."* Critically, the docking and meta-analyses are reported as *completed* results (Tables 1–3, Figs 1–5), not "to be done."
【Why it matters】 The grant-aim discipline requires the FJNSF AIMS to lock on STRETCH validation (clinical qPCR/ELISA, Visium, MD) and must NOT present the MVP bioinformatics screening as prospective. The manuscript satisfies this: the screen is presented as finished, the validation as the defined next step. The only residual danger is that a panel seeing "preliminary foundation for a pending grant" + the ADRA2A overlap infers the screening *is* the proposal. A single boundary sentence removes that inference.
【Specific fix】 Add to the Discussion (after line 115): *"The in-silico discovery and honest-null screening reported here are complete and constitute the preliminary foundation for a pending Fujian Natural Science Foundation application; the grant's proposed AIMS are the prospective wet-lab confirmation of the 35 hubs (plasma qPCR/ELISA, Visium spatial validation, and mechanistic follow-up), which are distinct from and build upon — rather than repeat — the computational work presented in this manuscript."*
