# Cover Letter — Scientific Reports

**Manuscript title:** Conserved nerve-injury-associated transcriptional response on the dorsal root ganglion–spinal axis: non-predictive incision translation and an honest repurposing null

**Corresponding author:** Yang Y, The Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Fuzhou, Fujian 350003, China. ORCID 0009-0004-9698-6552. Email: 960856791@qq.com

**To the Editorial Board of Scientific Reports,**

I submit the original research article above for consideration as an Article. This is a purely computational, multi-dataset dissection of the chronic postsurgical pain (CPSP) dorsal root ganglion–spinal cord axis, in which a prospectively specified, honest-null structure-based drug-repurposing screen is reported explicitly as a methodological boundary.

**Why Scientific Reports is the right venue.** Scientific Reports publishes sound, negative-result and methodological work that advances reproducibility—exactly the contribution here. Our central result is a convergently defined, *nerve-injury-associated* neuroimmune–metabolic axis on the DRG–spinal cord (coordinated DAM-like microglial and complement activation, plus—under the fixed-effect analysis only—OXPHOS suppression that does not survive random-effects correction, q = 0.31), with candidate hub genes nominated by a dual-machine-learning consensus under leave-one-dataset-out evaluation (whose generalisation is itself bounded). This coexists with specific nociceptor-channel down-regulation (SCN9A/SCN10A/SCN11A/SCN8A individually significant and down-regulated in nerve-injury models, but inverted in the single incision model). We pair this with an *honest null* from a prospectively specified full-library docking screen of 3,085 FDA drugs against 10 tractable targets, which found no target with robust, size-independent enrichment (ADRA2A Tier-1 AUC 0.618 collapsed to 0.532, p = 0.118; known analgesics not enriched). We report this as an explicit methodological boundary rather than a false-positive hit, and we show how inadequate controls produce double-dipping false positives. Such transparency aligns with the journal's reproducibility mandate.

**Honest reporting of fragility, stated up front.** Four of the five DRG-axis studies are nerve-injury models and only one incision arm is available, so we frame the axis as a nerve-injury-associated response and not a CPSP-specific pathway. Three further limits are reported as results rather than buried: (i) under a DerSimonian–Laird random-effects sensitivity analysis the core signature falls from 4,055 to 1,008 genes and OXPHOS suppression no longer survives set-level Benjamini–Hochberg correction; (ii) 57.3% of the fixed-effect core is not recovered in a bulk-only meta-analysis excluding the ribosome-profiling study; and (iii) the docking target set is not statistically reproducible under bootstrap (a median of 1 of 17 dock-eligible hubs recovered per resample), so target selection is justified by structural tractability rather than by hub-selection stability.

**Novelty and significance.** To our knowledge this is a CPSP-adjacent study that (i) integrates DRG and spinal-cord transcriptomes as a single axis via multi-dataset Stouffer meta-analysis with real-sample-size weighting, with a random-effects sensitivity analysis and τ²/I² heterogeneity reported alongside; (ii) nominates candidate hub genes by a dual-ML consensus under cross-dataset leave-one-dataset-out evaluation, with the stability of the derived target set bootstrapped explicitly; (iii) applies set-level Benjamini–Hochberg correction across gene sets, which the prior repurposing and pathway literature routinely omits; and (iv) reports a prospectively specified full-library FDA repurposing screen with reverse positive controls that yields no target clearing both filters (raw/full-library MW-corrected and size-independent MW-adjusted), i.e. an honest null. Most importantly, we show by a non-circular test that the nerve-injury signature *does not predict* incision direction (46.2% agreement versus a 47.1% background; difference −0.9 pp, permutation p = 0.14), which bounds how far nerve-injury transcriptomics can be extrapolated to postsurgical pain. Because no target cleared our own filters, we draw no therapeutic priority from the screen.
