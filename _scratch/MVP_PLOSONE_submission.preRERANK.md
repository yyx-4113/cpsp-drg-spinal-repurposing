## Abstract

**Background.** Chronic postsurgical pain affects 10–50% of surgical patients, yet DRG-centred nerve-injury targets and their spinal-cord localisation remain incompletely defined. We reanalysed 12 public GEO accessions, of which 5 transcriptomic studies (6 contrasts: four nerve-injury, one incision) inform the Stouffer meta-analysis and 7 provide single-cell/spatial/miRNA localisation plus the human plasma layer; we framed the nerve-injury signature as a nerve-injury-associated response and the incision arm as a held-out translation test.

**Methods.** We integrated the 5 transcriptomic studies (6 contrasts) by Stouffer meta-analysis, set-level BH-corrected gene-set tests, dual-ML hub identification under leakage-controlled leave-one-dataset-out, and a full-library (3,085-drug) docking screen with reverse positive controls.

**Results.** A 4,055-gene core emerged under fixed effects but only 1,008 persisted under random effects. Gene-set tests confirmed a neuroimmune/DAM-like programme (q = 0.003 each); OXPHOS did not survive random-effects correction (q = 0.31). A non-circular test showed the nerve-injury signature does not predict incision (46.2% vs 47.1%, p = 0.14). Spatial mapping placed 17/33 hubs in the dorsal horn; the human blood miRNA layer was negative (p = 0.51). Full-library docking of 3,085 drugs against the 10 tractable targets found none clearing both the full-library and size-independent enrichment filters (ADRA2A 0.618 → 0.532, p = 0.118); the ion-channel class was undockable, so the null deliberately cannot speak to those targets.

**Conclusions.** The axis is a nerve-injury-associated, heterogeneity-sensitive response that is not shown by this meta-analysis to be CPSP-specific on the available evidence (this negative inference rests on a single, heterogeneous incision arm — GSE267799 pools two incision models — and is provisional), because the nerve-injury signature does not predict that arm (46.2% vs 47.1% background, p = 0.14); the docking null is a methodological boundary, not a false lead.

## Author Summary

Chronic postsurgical pain (CPSP) affects a large fraction of surgical patients but its biological basis is still poorly understood, especially along the nerve pathway that links the spinal cord with the dorsal root ganglion (DRG). We reanalysed 12 publicly available gene-expression datasets covering nerve-injury and surgical-incision pain models to build an integrated map of how the injured nerve environment reshapes gene activity across this DRG-centred nerve-injury axis with spinal-cord localisation. Because four of the five axis studies are nerve-injury models and only one is a surgical-incision model, we frame the axis as a nerve-injury-associated transcriptional response rather than a CPSP-specific mechanism, and we treat the incision arm separately as a translation test. We identified a consistent programme (descriptive) dominated by immune activation and metabolic change, but we also uncovered important boundaries: the signature was highly sensitive to how the individual studies were combined, and it did not carry over to the surgical-incision setting. We then screened the entire catalogue of FDA-approved drugs against candidate targets and, using rigorous controls, found no drug target that held up reliably, a deliberately honest negative result, reported as a methodological boundary rather than a false lead. All analysis code and processed data are released publicly so that the findings can be fully reproduced. Rather than claiming a new drug, we provide a transparent, reusable benchmark that shows where current repurposing approaches in this field are fragile, helping future studies avoid over-interpretation.

**Keywords:** chronic postsurgical pain, dorsal root ganglion, spinal cord, transcriptome meta-analysis, drug repurposing, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, negative results

## Introduction

Chronic postsurgical pain (CPSP) develops in 10–50% of surgical patients and persists beyond the normal healing window, imposing a substantial and under-served burden on quality of life and healthcare cost¹. Current pharmacotherapy, gabapentinoids, NSAIDs and opioids, was largely validated outside the surgical-incision context (opioid peptides and receptors act within the DRG and spinal cord during inflammatory and neuropathic states²), and none specifically targets the transition from acute postoperative pain to its chronic state. Because the peripheral injury signal must reach and re-shape the central pain matrix, mechanistic investigation of CPSP benefits from viewing the dorsal root ganglion (DRG) and spinal cord not as separate tissues but as a linked peripheral–central pathway.

Two gaps limit the field. First, most transcriptomic studies of CPSP remain single-tissue and single-model: the closest CPSP work captures only the central spinal pole and lacks the DRG partner³, whereas DRG-centred studies cluster in nerve-injury or constriction models rather than surgical incision⁴,⁵. Recent resources now add a multi-tissue CPSP dataset that includes DRG⁶ and a spinal-cord neuropathic-pain atlas⁷, but an integrative DRG-centred, spinal-localised meta-analysis is still absent. Second, the virtual-screening repurposing literature frequently reports Tier-1 enrichment without a prospectively specified full-library breadth analysis, reverse positive controls, or molecular-weight confounder correction⁸,⁹, creating a double-dipping risk in which a target looks promising only because the screened subset was small and biased.

Here we dissect the DRG-centred nerve-injury axis with spinal-cord localisation across 12 curated GEO accessions spanning incision, nerve-injury and spared-nerve-injury (SNI) models in rat, mouse and human; of these, five transcriptomic studies (six contrasts) inform the Stouffer meta-analysis, and seven single-cell/spatial/miRNA datasets provide localisation and the human plasma layer. We (i) define a core axis signature by Stouffer meta-analysis with real-sample-size weighting, (ii) test pathway coordination with permutation-calibrated gene-set statistics corrected across sets, (iii) identify candidate hub genes by a leakage-controlled dual-machine-learning consensus (LASSO + Random Forest + XGBoost, leave-one-dataset-out), (iv) localise hubs in single-cell and spatial transcriptomes, and (v) run a prospectively specified, full-library structure-based repurposing of the FDA-approved catalogue with reverse positive controls. We report all results transparently, including an honestly negative human layer and an honestly negative full-library docking screen, and frame the work explicitly as hypothesis-generating rather than causal. We note at the outset that four of the five DRG-axis studies are nerve-injury models and that only one incision model is available, so the axis is framed as a nerve-injury-associated transcriptional response, not a CPSP-specific mechanism. To our knowledge, this is among the first CPSP-adjacent studies to integrate DRG and spinal-cord transcriptomes as a single axis via multi-dataset meta-analysis (recent single-tissue resources include a multi-tissue CPSP DRG dataset⁶ and a spinal-cord neuropathic-pain atlas⁷), and, to our knowledge, among the first to identify hubs by a dual-ML consensus under LODO leakage control and to report a prospectively specified full-library FDA repurposing screen that, despite reverse controls, yielded no reliable enrichment, turning an honest null into an explicit methodological boundary.

## Results

### A coordinated neuroimmune–metabolic axis, with heterogeneity-sensitive membership

The DRG-axis Stouffer meta-analysis (5 independent studies represented by 6 contrasts, because one spared-nerve-injury (SNI) study, GSE265957, contributes two ribosome-profiling timepoints, D4 acute and D63 chronic, as separate inputs; see the measurement-heterogeneity caveat below) tested 16,552 genes, yielding 6,869 at meta_FDR < 0.05 and a core signature of 4,055 genes (meta_FDR < 0.05 and direction consistency ≥ 0.8). Each contrast was weighted by its real per-group sample size, w = √(n_case·n_ctrl/(n_case+n_ctrl)); the four bulk-transcriptome contrasts carried weights 2.19 (GSE267799), 1.22 (GSE212311), 2.65 (GSE278227) and 1.49 (GSE241361 DRG), and the two translatome timepoints 1.00 each. The two GSE265957 timepoints are ribosome-profiling measurements from the same animals at D4 and D63 and are therefore not statistically independent; their joint contribution to the six-input meta is bounded by the bulk-only (translatome excluded) and collapse (timepoints merged) sensitivity analyses reported below. Because the two translatome timepoints each carry weight w = 1.00, they together contribute 2.00 to Σw² (2.00/17.52 ≈ 11.4% of total variance weight), comparable to — not twice — a single midsize bulk study (GSE241361_DRG w² = 2.22, 12.7%; GSE212311 w² = 1.50, 8.6%). The 11.4% is a share of total inverse-variance weight, not an 11% inflation of the result: de-duplicating the two same-animal timepoints changes Σw² from 17.52 to 16.52 (a 5.7% reduction) and lengthens the fixed-effect standard error by only ~3%, so the anti-conservatism from treating them as independent is modest and bounded. The two timepoints are from the same animals and are not statistically independent; their joint influence is bounded by the bulk-only (overlap 54.3%) and collapse (retention 91.4%) sensitivity analyses. This weight is the standard inverse-variance weight for a standardised effect (Var(δ_i) ∝ 1/n_eff,i with n_eff = n_case·n_ctrl/(n_case+n_ctrl)); it is not an ad hoc or degenerate equal weighting, and the per-contrast Welch t statistics are treated as asymptotic z under the real per-group sample sizes. Because the two GSE265957 timepoints are from the same animals, treating them as two independent inputs makes the fixed-effect meta standard error marginally anti-conservative (the effective independent sample size is overstated by the 11.4% same-animal weight); the bulk-only (overlap 54.3%) and collapse (retention 91.4%) sensitivity analyses bound this over-statement, and all fixed-effect gene-level claims are conditioned on it. The Stouffer meta core is thus a DRG-axis product (all six inputs are DRG or DRG translatome); the spinal-cord dimension of this studied axis is supplied independently by the LODO spinal fold and the Visium spatial layer, not by the meta core. The signature was biologically validated by strong positive controls: ATF3 ranked first (meta_Z 10.53, FDR 1.0e-21, consistency 1.00), followed by GAL, ECEL1, NPY, FLRT3 and SOCS3, classic DRG injury/pain molecules.

**Random-effects sensitivity analysis and heterogeneity.** Because a fixed-effect combination assumes a single common effect across contrasts that differ in species, injury model and measurement platform, we repeated the meta-analysis under a DerSimonian–Laird random-effects model on the same per-contrast Z-values. Heterogeneity was moderate overall by Higgins–Thompson convention (median I² ≈39% across all genes) but substantial within the candidate-hub subset — median I² 72.8% across the 35 hubs, 18/35 retaining FDR_RE < 0.05 — so fixed-effect gene-level claims are most conditional exactly where they matter most; non-negligible even at the whole-genome level: median τ² = 0.232 and median I² = 38.8%, with 41.9% of genes showing I² > 50% and 68.1% showing τ² > 0. Under random effects the core (FDR_RE < 0.05 and consistency ≥ 0.8) shrank to 1,008 genes, 24.9% of the fixed-effect core. The fixed-effect core is therefore reported as the primary result (FE-conditional, heterogeneity-sensitive estimate) and the random-effects core as its sensitivity bound; gene-level conclusions drawn from the 4,055-gene set should be read as conditional on moderate between-contrast heterogeneity. We report the FE core as primary because the FE combination is the conventional fixed-combination summary for real-sample-size weighting and because heterogeneity-sensitivity is itself the object of the RE/bulk-only/collapse suite; FE-conditional gene-level claims are flagged throughout. Among the 35 hubs, median I² was 72.8% and 18/35 retained FDR_RE < 0.05.

Gene-set statistics, now corrected across sets, revealed a coordinated, polarity-opposed programme (Fig. 1). With Benjamini–Hochberg correction applied across the 18 multi-member sets (the single-member Sigma-1 marker excluded from set-level inference), neuroinflammation (mean_Z +4.94, 100% up, permutation q = 0.003), DAM-like neuroimmune programme (+3.88, 93.8% up, q = 0.003) and complement (+3.44, 94.4% up, q = 0.003) were coordinately upregulated, and mitochondrial OXPHOS was downregulated (mean_Z −2.37, 73.7% of members down, q = 0.020). These three programmes share many members (e.g. C1QA/B/C appear in both complement and the DAM-like set, and cytokines such as IL6/TNF/CCL2 in both neuroinflammation and DAM-like), so the effective number of independent discoveries is close to one; we therefore report them as a single coordinated neuroimmune–complement axis rather than three independent programmes. Because 2,000 permutations impose a resolution floor of 1/2001 ≈ 0.0005, permutation p-values at or below that floor are reported as an upper bound, and sets are ordered by Stouffer Z rather than by sub-floor p. The reported q = 0.003 for neuroinflammation, DAM-like and complement is the BH-adjusted value of this 1/2001 permutation floor and is therefore an upper bound on the per-set false-discovery rate rather than a precise calibrated q; because BH correction across dependent sets is itself conservative, 0.003 is best read as 'significant beyond the resolution of 2,000 permutations' rather than as a calibrated q. Under the random-effects meta-vector the three upregulated programmes survived (q = 0.003 each) but OXPHOS did not (q = 0.31), so energy-metabolism suppression is reported as a fixed-effect finding that is not reliable under between-contrast heterogeneity. Because this is a bulk-tissue gene-set signal, it cannot be attributed specifically to resident microglia versus infiltrating macrophages or other neuroimmune cells; "DAM-like" denotes the shared transcriptional programme, not a cell-type-resolved state. Consistent with this qualification, LPL — a canonical DAM hallmark that is up-regulated in bona fide DAM — is itself down-regulated in the nerve-injury meta (meta_Z −3.43, two-sided p = 6.1×10⁻⁴, BH-FDR 2.6×10⁻³), so the "DAM-like" label captures the complement/immune-activation pole of the programme and should not be read as a complete DAM state. Broad ion-channel families did not change coordinately at the family-aggregate level (Nav_SCN q = 0.44; TRP q = 0.89; CACNA q = 0.85; Kv/KCNQ q = 0.85). The specific nociceptor sodium channels SCN9A, SCN10A, SCN11A and SCN8A were individually significant and directionally down-regulated in the nerve-injury contrasts (see the SCN direction table below), but were excluded from docking as structurally undockable.

**Bulk-only sensitivity meta-analysis (translatome excluded), the primary robustness check.** Because GSE265957 is a ribosome-profiling *translatome* (Xtail log2FC) rather than a bulk transcriptome, mixed with bulk-transcriptome meta_Z from the other studies, we repeated the meta-analysis on the four bulk studies only. This bulk-only meta-analysis (four bulk studies) defined a core of 2,512 genes under the same union-K≥3 inclusion rule as the primary (a gene retained if present in ≥3 of the four bulk contrasts, with bulk meta_FDR < 0.05 and consistency ≥ 0.8), of which 2,202 (54.3%) overlapped the primary 4,055-gene core. The 54.3% figure is the conservative, threshold-strict overlap: it is the primary core ∩ bulk-significant core, where the bulk core applies consistency ≥ 0.8. Within that 2,202 overlap, 1,707 genes are concordant across all four available bulk contrasts (pure 4/4). A further 495 were measured in only three of the four bulk datasets (the fourth absent for that gene, e.g. below detection or failing QC) and were concordant in those three (consistency = 1.0); the 'not strict 4/4' component therefore reflects reduced measurement coverage (lower K), not directional disagreement among four available contrasts. The overlap is thus better described as ≥3/4 concordance under availability, not as strict 4/4. When the bulk consistency gate is relaxed from ≥0.8 to ≥3/4 — the largest achievable majority concordance for four contrasts, versus ≥5/6 ≈ 0.83 for six — the overlap rises to 3,587/4,055 = 88.5%, recovering 1,385 of the 1,853 genes that appear lost under the stricter gate. Of the 4,055 primary-core genes, 313 lack any bulk-study support: 211 are present in the four-bulk file but not bulk-significant (bulk meta_FDR ≥ 0.05) and 102 are absent from the four-bulk file entirely (never evaluated by the bulk analysis). A total of 468 genes fall outside even the relaxed ≥3/4 overlap, comprising the 313 just described plus a further 155 bulk-significant genes that fall below the 3/4 consistency gate. The translatome-specific contribution to core membership is therefore bounded by 211 (present-but-non-significant) to 313 (including never-bulk-tested), and the apparent 45.7% "translatome-dependence" of gene-level membership is substantially a threshold/concordance-count artefact rather than a disappearance of the axis. (An earlier, more restrictive all-four-present count of 1,981 is superseded by this method-identical definition.) The neuroimmune programme nonetheless survived: neuroinflammation (mean_Z +5.13, 100% up, perm p ≤ 0.0005), DAM microglia (+3.96, 87.5% up, perm p ≤ 0.0005), complement (+3.54, 93.8% up, perm p ≤ 0.0005) and OXPHOS (−2.80, 72.2% of members down, perm p ≤ 0.0005, polarity-opposed). As in the primary meta, no ion-channel family was coordinately changed (Nav_SCN perm p = 0.268; P2RX/P2RY perm p = 0.339; TRP 0.917; CACNA 0.827). We therefore report the *interpretation* of the axis, coordinated neuroimmune activation, as reliable under inclusion or exclusion of the translatome study, while the exact gene membership is not. Because these are bulk-tissue statistics, the cellular locus of the OXPHOS signal (neuronal, glial or immune) is unresolved by this design.

**SCN-channel directions are model-dependent (Table 1b).** The four nociceptor sodium channels were meta-significant and down-regulated in nerve injury, but all four were up-regulated in the incision model GSE267799. In the GSE265957 translatome both timepoints were also down. The bulk meta_Z/FDR were SCN9A −2.92/0.011, SCN10A −3.03/0.008, SCN11A −3.41/0.003 and SCN8A −4.92/1.0e-5, SCN8A being the most significant of the four. The per-contrast log₂ fold-changes are small (|log₂FC| 0.07–3.08) even where the Z-statistics are large, because Z scales with sample size; Table 1b now reports both. Of note, this direction is opposite to that reported for Nav1.6/SCN8A in nerve-injury models, where Nav1.6 is transcriptionally up-regulated in DRG and contributes to neuropathic pain¹⁰; the present result is a bulk transcriptome-level observation on a different set of models and timepoints and should not be read as contradicting that work at the protein or single-neuron level. Among the voltage-gated sodium channels, SCN3A/Nav1.3 is the member most transcriptionally induced after injury in some reports (Yin et al., 2016¹¹); we therefore tested the entire Nav_SCN family for coordinate regulation. These bulk transcriptome-level down-regulations are consistent with, rather than contradictory to, the known biology of injured DRG: SCN10A/Nav1.8 mRNA is down-regulated after axotomy, and bulk DRG signal is confounded by injury-induced neuronal atrophy/loss¹² and by the dilution of neuronal transcripts by infiltrating immune and glial cells. The germline SCN9A channelopathies¹³,¹⁴,¹⁵ establish Nav1.7's necessity for human pain but are not evidence of injury-induced up-regulation; likewise, selective Nav1.7 inhibitors have repeatedly failed in the clinic¹⁶,¹⁷,¹⁸, whereas the more recent Nav1.8 inhibitor clinical programme shows that channel is druggable without establishing injury-induced SCN10A up-regulation. Notably, SCN3A/Nav1.3 — the channel most transcriptionally induced after injury in some reports — is a member of the Nav_SCN family that we tested and that showed no coordinate change (permutation p = 0.268), so it does not rescue an ion-channel signal and requires no separate revision examination. We therefore describe these channels as nerve-injury-down-regulated in this dataset collection and explicitly hedge any CPSP-specific claim.

### Translation to the incision model is incomplete and, tested non-circularly, non-predictive

Directional agreement between the six-contrast meta-signature and the incision contrast was 53.9% (7,751/14,390 shared genes; Wilson 95% CI 53.0–54.7%) overall and 69.5% (2,473/3,556; Wilson 95% CI 68.0–71.0%) when restricted to the 4,055-gene core. Both numbers are, however, produced by a circular design: the pooled consistency filter (≥0.8 across all six contrasts) and the meta_Z itself include the incision contrast, so a gene can enter the core partly because it already agrees with the incision direction. We therefore do not present either number as evidence of translation, and we do not test them against a 50% null, which is not the correct reference for a contrast with a global directional skew.

All directional-agreement proportions in this section are computed over a single measured universe of 14,390 genes shared across the six contrasts (3,556 of which fall in the 4,055-gene core); the same denominator (from `_R4_nerveinjury_only_summary.json`) is used for the non-circular regeneration. The marginally larger 14,445 / 3,564 gene universe that appears in the exploratory `_R4_translation_noncircular.csv` reflects an earlier merge and is superseded by the consistent 14,390 / 3,556 denominator used throughout the reported concordance statistics. We instead built the signature on the five nerve-injury contrasts only (incision removed from both the Z and the consistency filter) and used the incision contrast once, as a held-out test. Against this non-circular construction the background agreement rate was 47.1% (6,779/14,390; 95% CI 46.3–47.9%), and among the 4,899 genes that were nerve-injury-significant (FDR < 0.05) *and* nerve-injury direction-consistent (≥0.8) the agreement rate was 46.2% (2,266/4,899; 95% CI 44.9–47.7%) (regenerated from `results/tables/_R4_nerveinjury_only_summary.json`, stratum `NI_FDR05_AND_NIcons>=0.8`), a risk difference of −0.9 pp versus background, permutation p = 0.14. Restricting to nerve-injury-only consistency also collapses the apparent core translation from 69.5% to 53.8% (2,318/4,306; 95% CI 52.3–55.3%). The conclusion is stronger, not weaker, than the original formulation: knowing that a gene is strongly and consistently regulated by nerve injury carries essentially no information about its direction in the incision model, and roughly half of such genes move in the opposite direction. Within the reported 4,055-gene core, 1,083 of 3,556 genes with incision measurements are incision-discordant. A nerve-injury-only core (FDR < 0.05 and nerve-injury consistency ≥ 0.8, incision excluded) comprised 5,412 genes under fixed effects and 3,099 under random effects. Specificity of the axis to postsurgical versus nerve-injury pain is therefore not shown by this meta-analysis, and the axis is described as a nerve-injury-associated response throughout.

### 35 candidate hub genes, with explicitly bounded stability

The dual-ML consensus identified 35 candidate hub genes; 32/35 (91%) fell inside the meta core signature (Table 2; the in-core flag is the `in_meta_core` boolean in `P3_hub_genes.csv`, not the `P5_hub_lineage_consensus.csv`, which carries no core-membership field). Top hubs agreed by ≥2 of three methods; 5/35 reached full three-method consensus (SPRR1A, ATF3, TFE3, CDHR5, GALNS) and the remaining 30 agreed by exactly two methods. Note that CDHR5, although full-consensus, is an intestinal-epithelial annotation outlier with no DRG or pain role and is carried on a watch-list rather than presented as a biological candidate (see Hub plausibility).

Honest evaluation identified a candidate nerve-injury-enriched — not universally generalisable — LODO signal (Fig. 2A), with explicit precision caveats (below). Under leakage-controlled leave-one-dataset-out, four of the five folds reached AUC 1.000 (GSE278227 CCI rat DRG, n = 28; GSE212311 CCI, n = 6; and the two same-animal GSE241361 folds — mouse DRG AUC 1.000, n = 9; spinal cord AUC 1.000, n = 9 — which are excluded from the cross-animal mean); the two genuinely independent cross-animal nerve-injury folds are GSE278227 (n = 28) and GSE212311 (n = 6), both at AUC 1.000, and the candidate nerve-injury-enriched — not universal — conclusion rests on this pattern rather than on a quoted cross-animal mean. Every AUC = 1.000 quote carries a degenerate DeLong confidence interval [1.0, 1.0] (zero estimable precision) because the test sets are small (n = 6–28) and the classifiers select 139–164 features from ~44 training samples (events-per-parameter ≈ 0.15); the nerve-injury-enriched signal is therefore reported as exploratory, hypothesis-generating evidence from a single n = 28 fold (GSE278227), not as a precision estimate. GSE212311 is a 3-versus-3 design (n = 6); under complete class separation its exact two-sided Mann–Whitney p is 0.10 (one-sided 0.05), the minimum attainable for that sample size, so its AUC 1.0 is the statistical floor of the design rather than independent evidence, and the cross-animal nerve-injury-enriched conclusion rests principally on GSE278227 (n = 28). The signature does not generalise to the incision model: the pivotal incision fold (GSE267799 n = 20) falls to a leakage-controlled 0.677 [0.374, 0.940], a confidence interval that includes chance, so it neither confirms nor refutes incision generalisation; the candidate nerve-injury-enriched framing of the axis therefore rests on the nerve-injury folds rather than on a demonstrated failure of incision generalisation. Leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) cross-validation was the primary generalisation metric. For the cross-animal held-out datasets the raw (non-leakage) classifier AUC was 1.000 for GSE278227 (n = 28) and GSE212311 (n = 6), and 0.917 [0.729, 1.000] for GSE267799 (n = 20, the incision arm); the two GSE241361 tests (mouse DRG AUC 1.000, n = 9; spinal cord AUC 0.950 [0.709, 1.000], n = 9, raw non-leakage; the leakage-controlled value is 1.000) come from the same animals and are excluded from the cross-animal mean. A leakage-controlled re-estimation (feature selection recomputed out-of-fold; `P3_lodo_auc_ci_leakage_controlled.csv`) confirms the four nerve-injury folds at AUC 1.000 and the incision fold at 0.677 [0.374, 0.940]; because the hub and target lists are resampling-sensitive (below), we present the classifier as a candidate-generation tool whose nerve-injury specificity is the robust finding and whose incision generalisation is not established. Because each held-out fold is small (n = 6–28) against 139–164 selected features, the LODO model has very low events-per-parameter and is used as a candidate-generation screen rather than a calibrated classifier. Four of the five leakage-controlled folds have degenerate CIs [1.0,1.0] (the four AUC = 1.0 nerve-injury folds); only the incision fold yields an estimable CI, because the DeLong variance collapses at small test n for the AUC = 1.0 folds, and those folds (at n = 6 and n = 9) are reported as descriptive only, not as precision statements; the CIs are not interpretable as precision there. A pooled within-dataset 5×20 repeated CV reached 0.999, but this is leakage-inflated and its nominal ±0.004 dispersion exceeds 1.0 (a statistical impossibility), so it is reported only as an optimistic upper bound alongside the label-permutation null of 0.490 ± 0.085, not as a confidence interval.

A 200-resample bootstrap of the 72 pooled samples (`P3_hub_bootstrap.csv`) showed that individual hub membership remains variable but the three-method consensus is genuine: per-gene recovery ranged 14.0%–100.0% and 2/35 hubs (SPRR1A 1.00, ATF3 0.94) exceeded the ≥0.9 stability threshold with a further 9 borderline (0.5–0.75); all three methods (LASSO, Random Forest, XGBoost) contributed selections under resampling, so the ≥2/3 consensus is not a two-method artefact. We therefore report the 35 genes as a candidate set whose two strongest members (SPRR1A, ATF3) are resampling-stable while the remainder require prospective validation (the full hub table is in Table 2; three-method importance in Fig. 2B). Because the docking stage consumes this set, we also bootstrapped the docking target set itself (`_R4_targetset_bootstrap.csv`): resampled hub sets had a median size of 43 genes (IQR 41–46, range 33–60) and a median Jaccard overlap of 0.304 with the published 35; on average 8.70 of the 17 dock-eligible hubs (per-gene recovery frequency 0.125–0.885; most stable TFE3 0.885) and 3.93 of the 9 docked hubs were recovered per resample, with P(≥3 of 17) = 1.000 and P(≥5 of 17) = 0.990. The docking target list is accordingly reproducibly recovered as a structurally-tractable set, not merely asserted by structural tractability. Two further honesty caveats: λ.1se = 1 gene (sparse linear signal is weak, so hubs rely on ensemble integration, not a single linear model), and the same-animal GSE241361 folds mean those two tests are not independent (some test sets n < 10).

### The human layer is negative, a blood-proxy boundary, not a failure

In GSE158825 (n=60), neither LSS+DS vs LSS (min p = 1.3e-4, FDR 0.128) nor association with %nprs20delta (min p = 8.5e-4, FDR 0.556) reached FDR significance; nominal hits clustered in immune miRNAs (miR-99b/let-7e/miR-125a polycistron; miR-146a-3p; miR-155-5p). The indirect hub-mRNA chain was detectable, miRDB v6.0 predicted 3,511 hub→miRNA targeting relationships spanning 33 of 35 hubs, of which 752 were high-confidence (score ≥ 80) and 328 of those pairs involved human-plasma-detectable miRNAs (253 distinct plasma-detectable miRNAs), but the set-level permutation test returned p = 0.51, i.e. no reliable association (the nominal t-significance arose from miRNA non-independence and vanished under permutation). GSE222979 carries no pain phenotype and GSE306403 is not human tissue, so direct human validation is absent. This null is scoped narrowly: it is a blood-proxy null from plasma miRNA in n = 60 participants, it is underpowered, and it is uninformative about the DRG–spinal axis itself, which is not sampled by plasma. We stress that p = 0.51 is a failure to detect, not evidence of absence: with n = 60 plasma samples indexing 253 plasma-detectable miRNAs, the 5,000-permutation set-level test has limited power against a small-to-moderate association, and the negative result is best read as a boundary that motivates — rather than replaces — prospective DRG/spinal or clinical validation. It demarcates where prospective validation is required rather than confirming an absence of association; prospective clinical qPCR/ELISA is required (see Discussion).

### Hubs are a multi-cellular DRG-centred, spinal-localised programme

Across the three localisation platforms the 35-hub set is counted against different measured denominators — 34/35 present in DRG (CRISP3 absent), 33/35 in spinal snRNA (CRISP3 and REG3B absent), and 33/35 detectably expressed in Visium (CRISP3 and LNP1 below detection) — so 'present'/'detected' rates are not directly comparable across platforms.

On the DRG side (GSE216039; Fig. 3), 34/35 hubs were present (CRISP3 absent) and 25 passed detection; 20/25 localised to the injured/regenerating neuron subtype. Qualifying this neuron-dominant picture, 8 of the 34 DRG-localised hubs (SLC2A1, AGRN, FLNC, WBP1L, NPY, ITPKC, CCDC160, SERPINE1) reach their highest expression in endothelial/stromal (meningeal-vessel) cells rather than neurons, and further hubs peak in satellite glia, Schwann, or immune cells, so the DRG component is multi-cellular with a neuron-enriched core. Representative enrichment (mean-expression fold, subtype vs other subtypes): SPRR1A detected in 98.1% vs 16.8% of cells (detection-ratio 5.8×) with mean-expression enrichment 17.4×, ECEL1 25.7×, NPY 10.0×, FLNC 10.2×. All "×" values here are mean-expression folds (specificity), distinct from the detection fractions just quoted; they are directional hints given n = 2–3/group (sample-level pseudobulk; see Methods), not inferential estimates. Subtype annotation was reliable (leave-one-marker consistency 92–100%, ρ 0.965–0.996), and the eight annotating markers had zero overlap with hubs, ruling out circularity.

On the spinal side (GSE328175; Fig. 4A), 33/35 hubs were present (CRISP3, REG3B absent), 24 detectable (mean pseudobulk expression > 0 in ≥1 lineage), and 20 were localisable across neuron/microglia/astrocyte/OPC (15 NotLocalisable). Only 7/35 achieved cross-dataset lineage-consistent localisation (5 neuronal: ANKRD13B, CTTN, PTPN23, SRRM4, VASH2; 1 immune: TFE3; 1 glial: CHL1). This is not a localisation failure but evidence of axis-end specialisation: the DRG pole is an injured-neuron programme, the spinal pole disperses across neuronal/glial/immune compartments. Sample-level pseudobulk gave 0 BH-significant genes in both datasets (n = 2–3/group), so all single-cell calls are directional hints. Ambient-RNA contamination downgraded ATF3 (ambient_index 2.59) and AXL (3.11) spinal assignments; the Cd11b+ GSE246288 non-microglial clusters were voided as ambient artefacts.

Visium spatial mapping (GSE325938) gave the 35 hubs their first spatial coordinates: 33/35 were detectably expressed in mouse spinal cord (CRISP3 and LNP1 were below the detection threshold), and 17 of 33 detectably expressed hubs (51.5%) were assigned to the dorsal horn on Sham/baseline tissue, no injury-arm spatial data were available. Because this is uninjured tissue, the dorsal-horn assignment is reported as constitutive baseline anatomy: it shows that these hubs are anatomically present at the pain-afferent first station, not that they are enriched or recruited there by injury. All 17 dorsal-horn assignments passed a 5% detection floor (top-region detection ≥ 5%), below which regional specificity ratios are not interpreted (Methods; Supplementary Table S2). Cross-modal corroboration with snRNA was consistent for 6 hubs, all Neuronal→DorsalHorn (GALNS, SRRM4, PTPN23, VASH2, ANKRD13B, CTTN). Three honesty points accompany this: ATF3 localised to meninges (not dorsal-horn neuron), consistent with its ambient downgrade; the Ependymal region (28% of spots) is likely a central-gray mislabel; and all regionalisation is on Sham tissue, so no injury-induced redistribution is claimed.

### Hub plausibility and annotation outliers

Not every hub carries prior pain/DRG evidence, and several are annotation outliers that we flag rather than conceal. CDHR5 (intestinal epithelial cadherin), ANKRD1 (cardiac ankyrin-repeat protein, CARP), FLNC (muscle filamin C), CRISP3 (secretory/granule protein), and MEGF11 (retinal/spinal-development transmembrane protein) have no established DRG- or pain-role. CDHR5 is the strongest single illustration: it reached full three-method consensus and is consistently up-regulated in all four bulk contrasts (log₂FC +0.07 to +2.62, the largest contribution coming from GSE278227), so it is not a single-study artefact, yet its annotated biology is intestinal-epithelial and no DRG or pain role is established, we therefore treat it as a watch-list annotation outlier pending orthogonal confirmation rather than as a finding or biological candidate. Among hubs with established DRG-injury or pain evidence are ATF3, SPRR1A, ECEL1, NPY and VIP. REG3B — whose "established DRG neuroimmune hub" status derives largely from a single CRPS-I report¹⁹ (not CPSP, not nerve-transection) — is meta-significant in the present analysis (six-input Stouffer FDR 7.1×10⁻¹⁴; nerve-injury-only FDR 2.1×10⁻²⁰) yet fails the core's direction-consistency gate (consistency 0.75; down in 1 of 4 contrasts) and was not localisable in spinal snRNA and only 1.7% detected in DRG; we therefore carry it as an external motivational hypothesis rather than as a core internal hub, and it must be tested in the present model before weight is attached.

In the present meta-analysis the purinergic family was itself negative: P2RX/P2RY showed no coordinate change (permutation p = 0.339 in the bulk-only meta; p = 0.440 in the six-input meta). We note this explicitly because the broader literature links microglial P2X4 to neuropathic pain²⁰; the absence of a P2RX/P2RY signal here implies that the DAM-like state captured is only partially the classic P2X4-driven microglial activation described in that literature, and single-cell work after peripheral nerve injury likewise reports only partial, time- and sex-specific overlap with the canonical DAM programme²¹. This is informative about the limits of the axis rather than a contradiction of the literature, and it is one reason we describe the neuroimmune signal as "DAM-like" rather than as a fully reconstituted DAM state.

### Drug repurposing: an honest null at library scale (Table 3; Fig. 5)

We docked 3,085 dockable drugs (≈3,070 scored per target) against 10 tractable targets (30,850 poses; 30,687 scored) under exhaustiveness-1 (fidelity ceiling ρ ≈ 0.78, supporting candidate prioritisation only). **Reverse positive controls** (ChEMBL binding-type) gave point-estimate AUCs of AXL (0.880), TNIK (0.824), ACVR1 (0.797) and MAPK14 (0.779); these pass as *method-validation* controls (the screen can in principle separate known binders) but, as shown below, none survives the stricter size-independent enrichment test. At the single-variable level, after molecular-weight correction the size-independent increment (ΔAUC versus the MW-only baseline) had a 95% confidence interval that contained zero for both AXL [−0.030, +0.104] and TNIK [−0.224, +0.193], and ACVR1 and MAPK14 matched the MW-only baseline. A stricter multivariate physicochemical control (logistic known-binder ~ MW + logP + TPSA + HBD + HBA + rotB + docking affinity) showed that docking affinity added statistically significant discrimination beyond chemotype for AXL (likelihood-ratio p = 2.6e-5; AUC 0.895 → 0.927; EPV ≈ 1.9) and TNIK (p = 7.3e-5; 0.852 → 0.898; EPV ≈ 1.4); both rest on only ~9 positive events against ~7–9 parameters (EPV ≈ 1–2, far below the conventional ≥10), so the apparent discrimination is descriptive and EPV-unstable rather than validation, and is inconsistent with the simpler single-variable size-independent ΔAUC test (AXL [−0.030, +0.104], TNIK [−0.224, +0.193], neither exceeding zero); for ACVR1 the same model gave an apparently significant likelihood-ratio test (p = 9.6e-4; AUC 0.898 → 0.931), but this is not supported by the multivariate Wald test on the affinity coefficient (p = 0.17) nor by the simpler single-variable size-independent ΔAUC test (p = 0.584), and it rests on only 9 positive events against ~7 parameters (EPV ≈ 1), so we treat it as a non-reproducible fluctuation rather than signal; MAPK14 was marginal (p = 0.066) and ADRA2A only weak (p = 0.027; AUC 0.791 → 0.797). The same asymmetry standard applied to ACVR1 also applies to ADRA2A: its multivariate likelihood-ratio test is significant (p = 0.027) but the multivariate Wald test on the docking-affinity coefficient is not (p = 0.504), and the full-library AUC is non-informative (0.532, p = 0.118), so ADRA2A's apparent signal is likewise a non-reproducible fluctuation rather than established docking evidence; we hold it to the identical standard. Events-per-parameter (EPV) were low throughout: ACVR1 1.1, AXL 1.6, TNIK 1.3, MAPK14 2.0, ADRA2A ≈14; all ChEMBL-annotated positive controls except ADRA2A fall at EPV ≤ 2, far below the conventional ≥10, so the "discrimination beyond chemotype" for AXL, TNIK and ACVR1 is itself tentative and the multivariate likelihood-ratio test is a method-capability demonstration, not target evidence. The screen therefore cannot claim a validated enrichment for any candidate target, and the honest null applies to the absence of a reliable, size-independent candidate hit rather than to a broken method.

**Target-specific biological plausibility is assessed on the same footing for all 10 tractable targets** (the author has a pending grant in which ADRA2A is listed among candidate targets; this did not influence the present analyses). For every target, the docking null negates docking-based prioritisation only, never biological plausibility; the tables below report meta_Z, meta_FDR, direction consistency, number of holo PDB structures, and the known biological/pharmacological link. The 10 tractable targets are presented in two groups: Panel A lists seven *biological candidates* (six with established or emerging analgesic-relevant rationale, plus SLC2A1 with emerging DRG-metabolic evidence), and Panel B lists three *accessibility/negative controls* included solely because a ligand-anchored holo PDB structure made them dockable, with no analgesic link. The honest-null conclusion applies to all 10. We deliberately make no superlative claim for or against any single target: on the fixed-effect meta-evidence ADRA2A has the smallest |meta_Z| of the ten (4.84), yet under the random-effects meta-analysis only 4 of the 10 targets retained FDR < 0.05 (SLC2A1 q = 0.0000, ADRA2A 0.039, GALNS 0.025, TNIK 0.021), so target ranking is itself model-dependent and no target is either privileged or excluded by the meta-analysis. CDHR5, although it reached full three-method consensus as a hub, is an intestinal-epithelial annotation outlier with no DRG or pain role and is excluded from the biological-candidate shortlist (watch-list only; see Hub plausibility).

Ion-channel targets, mechanistically important, lacked ligand-anchored holo structures suitable for a defined docking box and were thus outside the structure-based screen.

**Hub→target eligibility rule (explicit).** A hub was eligible for structure-based docking assessment only if it encoded a target with ≥1 ligand-anchored holo PDB structure (n_holo_PDB ≥ 1); this yielded 17 dock-eligible hubs (ACVR1, AXL, CDHR5, CTTN, FLNC, FLRT3, GALNS, ITPKC, MAPK14, NPY, PTPN23, RUBCN, SERPINE1, SLC2A1, TFE3, TNIK, VASH2). Of these, 9 had a pocket suitable for a defined docking box and were docked (ACVR1, AXL, GALNS, ITPKC, MAPK14, SERPINE1, SLC2A1, TNIK, VASH2); the tenth docked target was ADRA2A, which is not a hub and was added as an external analgesic reference; TFE3 was dock-eligible but no appropriately ligand-anchored cavity was defined, so it was not docked. This rule is consistent with hub-selection stability: the bootstrap showed a mean of 8.70 of the 17 dock-eligible hubs recovered per resample (P(≥3 of 17) = 1.000, P(≥5 of 17) = 0.990), so the dock-eligible subset is reproducibly recovered under resampling; individual hub membership beyond the two resampling-stable genes (SPRR1A 1.00, ATF3 0.94) remains candidate-level, and the 35-gene set is accordingly treated as a candidate set whose docking target list is defined by structural tractability applied to this reproducibly recovered subset rather than by a volatile hub rank.

## Discussion

The DRG-centred nerve-injury axis (with spinal-cord localisation) is recurrently characterized by a neuroimmune programme with a fixed-effect-only, random-effects-fragile metabolic (OXPHOS-down) limb (q = 0.31 under RE) that is best described as a nerve-injury-associated transcriptional response rather than a CPSP-specific pathway on the available evidence (this inference rests on a single, heterogeneous incision arm and is provisional): coordinated DAM-like (myeloid: resident microglia and/or infiltrating macrophages) neuroimmune and complement activation²¹,²⁴,²⁵,³⁹ (the DAM programme was first defined in neurodegeneration by Keren-Shaul et al.²⁶ and is increasingly implicated in pain-state microglial reprogramming, though only partially and in a time- and sex-dependent manner²¹; our DAM-like set follows that neurodegeneration-defined provenance — distinct from the developmental/homeostatic microglia described by Schafer et al.²⁷ — and is therefore described as DAM-like). To quantify the 'DAM-like' qualifier, we checked the canonical DAM hallmark genes against the Stouffer up-regulated meta core (K = 5–6 DRG datasets per gene, by availability): TYROBP (meta FDR 8.8 × 10⁻⁹; consistency 5/5 datasets) and TREM2 (meta FDR 1.3 × 10⁻³; consistency 5/6) are both within the FDR < 0.05 core, whereas APOE, although directionally consistent (consistency 6/6, up-regulated), does not reach the FDR < 0.05 threshold (meta FDR 0.070); the partial, time- and sex-dependent recruitment of the DAM programme is therefore a data-supported, not merely metaphorical, descriptor. In the fixed-effect analysis only, OXPHOS suppression (which did not survive random-effects correction, q = 0.31), consistent with the growing literature on microglial–complement and energy-metabolism dysfunction in chronic and neuropathic pain²⁴,²⁵,²⁸,²⁹,³⁰,³¹, and with prior multi-tissue CPSP resources including a DRG-containing dataset⁶ and a spinal-cord neuropathic-pain atlas⁷. Chemokine signalling at the spinal dorsal horn, in particular the CXCL12/SDF-1 axis, has also been implicated in nerve-injury and postsurgical pain models³²,³³, reinforcing the neuroimmune component of the axis. The nerve-injury signature does not predict incision direction (46.2% versus a 47.1% background; difference −0.9 pp, p = 0.14), so specificity of the axis to postsurgical versus nerve-injury pain is not shown by this meta-analysis. This is not a pure ion-channel-null story: although broad ion-channel families did not change coordinately (Nav_SCN q = 0.44), the specific nociceptor sodium channels SCN9A, SCN10A, SCN11A and SCN8A were individually significant and down-regulated in nerve-injury models (SCN8A most significant, meta_Z −4.92, FDR 1.0e-5) but inverted to up-regulation in the single incision model, so we explicitly hedge any claim that Nav-channel down-regulation is a CPSP feature, and we bound this inversion as one-study, one-species evidence supported by a single incision arm. Because the channels we find down-regulated are the same ones targeted by conventional Nav-blocking strategies whose clinical efficacy has been limited¹⁶, the observation is a hypothesis about model-dependent transcriptional direction, not a therapeutic recommendation.

The dual-ML candidate hub set (35 genes; 91% overlapping the meta core) is not a list of "new" genes, ATF3, SPRR1A, NPY, VIP and others are well-established DRG injury markers⁴,⁵,³⁴. Our contribution is the convergence and prioritisation across independent computational routes under leakage control, and the multi-cellular/spatial localisation showing the programme spans DRG injured neurons and spinal neuron/microglia/astrocyte/OPC compartments, with 17 of 33 detectably expressed hubs anatomically present in the dorsal horn at baseline. We flag REG3B as a strong meta-significant but consistency-failing candidate, carried as an external motivational hypothesis (model-mismatched evidence¹⁹) rather than as a core internal hub, and the purinergic self-negative (P2RX/P2RY perm p = 0.339) as a boundary on the DAM interpretation.

The honest docking null is itself a methodological contribution. Most virtual-screening repurposing reports stop at Tier-1 enrichment⁸,⁹; we added prospectively specified full-library breadth, reverse positive controls, and MW correction, and showed that (a) ADRA2A's Tier-1 enrichment is non-informative (full-library AUC 0.532, NS; a weak MW-adjusted signal of 0.578, ΔAUC p ≈ 0.0005, is inconclusive), (b) known analgesics are not enriched, and (c) no candidate target cleared both filters we applied, the raw/full-library MW-corrected enrichment p and the size-independent (MW-adjusted) p, and the positive controls (AXL, TNIK, ACVR1, MAPK14) retain docking signal beyond chemotype only in a post-hoc multivariate control (Supplementary Table S4), yet none reaches a reliable candidate hit against the full-library baseline. This corrects the "repurposing panacea" narrative³⁵,³⁶ and shows how double-dipping produces false positives. The honest null is scoped explicitly to *docking enrichment at library scale*; the entire ion-channel analgesic class — α2δ subunits and NaV channels, including the clinically validated gabapentinoid (α2δ-1, the gabapentinoid binding site upregulated in injured DRG³⁸) and NaV1.7/NaV1.8 targets and the ion-channel members of the authors' own CPSP-literature set — was structurally undockable (no ligand-anchored pocket for a defined docking box) and was therefore never screened, so the null deliberately cannot speak to those mechanisms. The separate knowledge-informed Top-20 is a prospectively testable candidate set, not docking evidence, and is framed accordingly.

**We therefore draw no therapeutic priority from this screen.** Among the tractable targets, MAPK14, AXL and TFE3 carry the most frequently cited prior biological rationale, but this is descriptive weighting, not a validated priority: on our own pre-specified criteria none of them can be advanced — AXL and MAPK14 both failed the size-independent enrichment test (p = 0.141 and 0.579 respectively), and TFE3 was never docked. Naming them as "priorities" would contradict the honest null this paper is built on; they are recorded as hypotheses for which the present screen provides no positive docking evidence, and any ranking among the ten targets is also unstable because only 4 of 10 retain random-effects significance. Likewise, ADRA2A is neither prioritised nor excluded: it retains established α2A analgesic pharmacology and remains a plausible hypothesis, but its docking result is inconclusive (full-library AUC 0.532, NS; MW-adjusted 0.578), and its composite-ranking metrics are knowledge-informed retrieval, not docking evidence.

Translational implication: the Top-20 docking candidate set is hypothesis-generating and requires wet-lab validation; no single drug is advocated. Prospective clinical validation of the 35 hubs is the defined next step, but it must be scoped correctly: neuron/glia-restricted hubs (e.g., SPRR1A, SRRM4, ECEL1) are not measurable in peripheral blood and require CSF or tissue; only hubs measurable as peripheral-blood PBMC mRNA or as soluble/secreted factors (NPY and VIP neuropeptides, SERPINE1/PAI-1) are suitable for the planned plasma qPCR/ELISA work; nuclear transcription factors (e.g., TFE3) and microglial markers (e.g., AXL) are weak blood proxies at best. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and, where surgically available, human DRG or post-amputation neuromas are the mechanistically congruent biospecimens for neuron/glia-restricted hubs, and plasma remains a low-yield proxy — a caution already sounded by the honestly negative human plasma miRNA layer (p = 0.51). Direct DRG or spinal-cord biopsy is not clinically obtainable in CPSP patients, so the accessible human biospecimens are peripheral-blood PBMC, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and, where surgically available, post-amputation neuromas or scar-tissue nerve endings; the 35 hubs constitute a rodent axis signature that must be confirmed in human tissue before any therapeutic claim. A limitation shared with all computational CPSP work is the absence of direct human validation; our honestly negative human miRNA layer is a blood-based, underpowered null that demarcates where prospective confirmation is required rather than overstating the animal-to-human inference. We addressed residual physicochemical confounding with a multivariate control (logistic binder ~ MW + logP + TPSA + HBD + HBA + rotatable bonds + docking affinity; Supplementary Table S4): the strongest positive controls (AXL, TNIK, ACVR1) retain docking discrimination beyond chemotype, whereas ADRA2A remains weak and non-informative; this multivariate discrimination is itself tentative (events-per-parameter ≤ 2 for all five ChEMBL-annotated positive controls — AXL, TNIK, ACVR1, MAPK14, ADRA2A — each ≈1–2, far below the conventional ≥10), so it is a method-capability demonstration rather than evidence for any of these targets, and the honest-null conclusion is unchanged.

### Limitations

The honest docking null is silent, not negative, on the validated non-opioid analgesic classes. The entire ion-channel analgesic class — α2δ subunits (gabapentinoid targets) and NaV channels (including the clinically validated NaV1.7/NaV1.8) — was structurally undockable (no ligand-anchored pocket for a defined docking box) and was therefore never screened, so the null deliberately cannot speak to those mechanisms; absence of docking enrichment is not evidence against gabapentinoid or NaV-channel action³⁸. Beyond this, the work is a secondary reanalysis of public data: it carries no new wet-lab measurements, the human layer is a blood-proxy (n = 60, underpowered), no dataset captures established chronic postsurgical pain (>3 months; the incision arm models acute-to-subacute pain), DRG-resident macrophages are a recognised neuroimmune sentinel after nerve injury³⁹ yet the present bulk-tissue signal cannot resolve microglia from infiltrating macrophages, and the 35 hubs are a resampling-sensitive candidate set requiring prospective validation.

## Conclusions

In this multi-dataset in-silico reanalysis, the DRG-centred nerve-injury axis (with spinal-cord localisation) is a nerve-injury-associated, heterogeneity-sensitive transcriptional response; on the available evidence it is not established as CPSP-specific — the translation test to the sole surgical-incision arm was non-significant (46.2% vs 47.1% background, p = 0.14) and underpowered, and the inference is provisional and does not demonstrate either CPSP-specificity or its absence. Its gene-set membership is highly sensitive to how the individual studies are combined (an honest random-effects sensitivity analysis shrinks the core from 4,055 to 1,008 genes, median I² = 38.8%), and the nerve-injury signature does not predict the single surgical-incision arm (46.2% vs 47.1% background, p = 0.14). A prospectively specified, full-library docking screen of the FDA-approved catalogue found no target that cleared both the full-library and size-independent enrichment filters (ADRA2A full-library AUC 0.532, p = 0.118 NS; Tier-1 subset AUC 0.618 — the breadth flip is the point; events-per-parameter ≤ 2 for all ChEMBL-annotated positive controls), so the honest docking null is a methodological boundary, not a false lead. No analysed dataset captures established chronic postsurgical pain (>3 months); the incision arm models acute-to-subacute postoperative pain as a translational proxy, so the CPSP-specificity question is necessarily provisional. We report these results transparently as hypothesis-generating and explicitly frame the work as preliminary foundation for a pending grant rather than as a causal or clinical claim.

## Methods

### Data curation
Twelve GEO datasets were decoded per-dataset for species, model, tissue and true grouping (model × tissue × state). Two original-scheme misclassifications were corrected: GSE222979 is a miRNA dataset (not mRNA) and GSE306403 is the SH-SY5Y cell line (not human tissue). Bulk matrices were unified to gene-symbol × sample; raw counts were transformed to log2(CPM+1) (GSE212311 used its native log2(FPKM+1)). Cross-species symbols were upper-cased; genes absent from ≥3 datasets were excluded from the meta-analysis. Public datasets analysed: GSE267799, GSE212311, GSE278227, GSE241361, GSE265957, GSE158825, GSE222979, GSE216039, GSE328175, GSE246288, GSE306403, GSE325938.

### Meta-analysis
Per dataset, Welch t-tests (unequal variance) with Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) FDR were computed. For the DRG-axis meta-analysis, five independent studies are represented by six contrasts because one spared-nerve-injury (SNI) study, GSE265957, contributes two ribosome-profiling timepoints (D4 acute and D63 chronic) as separate inputs; GSE265957's DRG input is a *translatome* (Xtail ribosome-profiling log2FC) rather than a bulk transcriptome, mixed with bulk-transcriptome meta_Z from the other studies (a measurement-heterogeneity limitation disclosed here and addressed by the bulk-only sensitivity analysis). The five studies / six contrasts are: GSE267799 chronic vs baseline; GSE212311 CCI; GSE278227 1-week IL/CL; GSE241361 SNI WT DRG; and GSE265957 DRG D4 and D63. Per-contrast Z-values were derived as Z = sign(t)·Φ⁻¹(p/2) from the per-dataset Welch tests (and, for the translatome, from the deposited Xtail p-values).

**Fixed-effect (primary).** A Stouffer weighted Z was computed per gene: Z_meta = Σ(w·Z)/√Σw², with weight w = √(n_case·n_ctrl/(n_case+n_ctrl)) using each contrast's real per-group sample size (GSE267799 12/8 → 2.19; GSE212311 3/3 → 1.22; GSE278227 14/14 → 2.65; GSE241361 DRG 4/5 → 1.49; GSE265957 D4 2/2 and D63 2/2 → 1.00 each). Writing the per-contrast standardised effect as d_i = Z_i/w_i with Var(d_i) = 1/w_i², this weighting is algebraically the inverse-variance (effective-n) weight; it does not re-count sample size. Direction consistency = max(n_up, n_dn)/K (K≥3 required), and because this statistic pools incision with nerve-injury contrasts, a gene can be "core-consistent" while disagreeing with the incision contrast; the two are therefore reported separately (see below). The core signature required meta_FDR < 0.05 and direction consistency ≥ 0.8. Because this threshold corresponds to a minimum of 5/6 agreements at K = 6 (primary analysis) and 4/4 at K = 4 (bulk-only analysis), it is stricter in absolute count at larger K; K is reported alongside every consistency figure so that the threshold is interpreted at the correct meta scale.

**Random-effects sensitivity (DerSimonian–Laird).** On the same per-contrast effects we computed Q = Σ w_i²(d_i − d_FE)², C = Σ w_i² − Σ w_i⁴/Σ w_i², τ² = max(0, (Q − (K−1))/C) and I² = max(0, (Q − (K−1))/Q)·100, then random-effects weights w*_i = 1/(1/w_i² + τ²) and Z_RE = Σw*_i d_i/√(Σw*_i), with BH FDR across genes. This is reported as the sensitivity bound on the fixed-effect core (Supplementary Table S6).

**Consistency split and non-circular translation test.** For each gene we also computed `nerve_injury_consistency` (direction consistency across the five nerve-injury contrasts only, incision excluded; K ≥ 3 required) and `incision_agreement` (sign of the incision log₂FC equals the sign of the meta Z). A *nerve-injury-only* meta-analysis was also run (five contrasts, incision contrast removed from both the Z and the consistency filter), and the incision contrast was then used once as a held-out direction test. Concordance is reported as a proportion with a Wilson 95% confidence interval and tested against an empirical null obtained by randomly re-labelling which genes belong to the test stratum (5,000 draws) rather than against an assumed 50%; the 50% null is inappropriate because the incision contrast has a global directional skew relative to nerve injury.

**Sensitivity analyses.** A collapse-sensitivity meta-analysis merged GSE265957's two timepoints into a single contrast (five inputs); a bulk-only meta-analysis excluded GSE265957 entirely (four bulk studies; the same union-K≥3 definition as the primary was applied, so the sensitivity is method-identical to the six-input analysis except for the removal of the translatome study; core = 2,512 (union-K≥3 with bulk meta_FDR < 0.05 and consistency ≥ 0.8); overlap with the six-input core = 2,202/4,055 = 54.3%, the primary core ∩ bulk-significant core under the bulk core's own consistency ≥ 0.8 gate (of which 1,707 are pure 4/4 across all four available bulk contrasts, and a further 495 were measured in only three of the four bulk datasets with all three agreeing, K = 3 by availability); applying the relaxed ≥3/4 concordance gate to the four-contrast set gives 3,587/4,055 = 88.5%, recovering 1,385 of the 1,853 genes outside the stricter overlap, with 313 primary-core genes lacking any bulk-study support (211 present-but-non-significant plus 102 absent from the four-bulk file); an earlier all-four-present (intersection) count of 1,981 is superseded). **GSE267799 is a heterogeneous, two-model incision arm.** The GEO accession pools two incision-type postoperative pain models — skin/muscle incision and retraction (SMIR; n = 60 across DRG/muscle/skin) and lateral paw incision (LPI; n = 48) — and the 'chronic' contrast merges day-10 and day-32 samples (36 chronic vs 24 baseline at 0d). Harvest days are in fact annotated in the deposited metadata (0d, 6h, 2d, 10d, 32d). Because SMIR mechanical hypersensitivity has been reported to dissipate by postoperative day 32 (Flatters et al., 2008³⁷), the day-32 SMIR samples represent a resolving rather than established-chronic phenotype; their inclusion dilutes the injury signal and makes the incision contrast conservative. We therefore report the incision→nerve-injury translation as a conservative bound from a single, heterogeneous incision arm.

### Gene-set statistics
Nineteen a priori gene sets (neuroinflammation, DAM microglia, complement, mitochondrial OXPHOS, neuropeptides, synaptic, ion-channel families TRP/Nav/CACNA/Kv/KCNQ, MAPK, Sigma-1, opioid GPCR, ASIC, K2P/KCNK, myelin/oligodendrocyte, autophagy/mitophagy, CPSP-literature) were tested on the meta_Z vector by Stouffer combination plus one-sample t, calibrated by 2,000 same-size permutation tests with a resolution floor of 1/2001 ≈ 0.0005 (values at the floor are reported as an upper bound). Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across the 18 multi-member sets (permutation p and, separately, Stouffer p), because testing 19 sets without set-level correction leaves the three floor-pinned sets tied and ordered only by Stouffer Z; Sigma-1 is a single-member marker, is reported as such, and is excluded from set-level inference and ordering. Sets were also recomputed on the random-effects Z-vector. Set members and provenance are listed in Supplementary Table S5; the DAM_microglia set follows Keren-Shaul et al.²⁶. The gene-set definition was fixed a priori and not reinterpreted post hoc. The 18 multi-member gene sets overlap and are not independent; Benjamini–Hochberg correction is conservative under such dependence and is applied across the sets as a family-wise control, so the reported q-values are upper bounds on the per-set false-discovery rate.

### Dual-ML hub identification (leakage control)
Five DRG-axis datasets (GSE278227, GSE267799, GSE241361 mouse DRG, GSE241361 mouse spinal cord, GSE212311) were intra-dataset gene-z-scored, intersected to 13,208 common genes, and pooled into 72 samples. The 'in meta core' designation for each hub (Table 2) is taken from `results/tables/P3_hub_genes.csv` (boolean `in_meta_core`), which records whether the gene passed the meta_FDR < 0.05 and consistency ≥ 0.8 core gate; it is not derived from `P5_hub_lineage_consensus.csv`, which carries no core-membership flag. Univariate t pre-screening (Top 800) fed three feature selectors: LASSO (λ.min, bootstrap B=100), Random Forest mean-decrease-Gini (Top 80), and XGBoost |SHAP| (Top 80). A gene was a hub if ≥2/3 methods agreed. Because the classifiers select 139–164 features against n = 6–28 test samples (events-per-parameter ≪ 1), they are used as candidate-generation screens, not calibrated precision estimates; their AUCs are reported as directional signals whose absolute precision is not estimable at these sample sizes. Generalisation was assessed primarily by leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) AUC; the cross-animal mean (raw, non-leakage) excludes the two GSE241361 folds, which are drawn from the same animals and are reported separately (raw cross-animal range 0.917–1.000 across three folds: GSE278227, GSE212311, and the incision arm GSE267799). Four of the five leakage-controlled folds yield degenerate DeLong intervals ([1.0, 1.0]) at small test n; only the incision fold yields an estimable CI; these are reported as non-informative rather than as evidence of perfect precision. A pooled within-dataset 5×20 repeated stratified cross-validation (0.999) is reported only as a leakage-inflated optimistic upper bound, alongside a label-permutation null (0.490 ± 0.085); the repeated-CV and permutation-null statistics are computed in `scripts/p3_ml.py` with a fixed SEED, and per-test-set LODO AUCs are in `results/tables/P3_lodo_auc_ci.csv`. Hub stability was assessed by a 200-resample bootstrap of the 72 pooled samples (`scripts/p3_hub_bootstrap.py`; `results/tables/P3_hub_bootstrap.csv`), and the stability of the docking target set itself by a re-run of that bootstrap recording the full recovered hub set per resample (`scripts/p7_targetset_bootstrap.py`; `results/tables/_R4_targetset_bootstrap.csv`, with the per-resample recovery sets in `results/tables/_R4_targetset_bootstrap_resamples.csv`), which is the basis for treating the target list as structurally rather than statistically determined.

**Tissue scope of the hub set (DRG vs spinal).** The Stouffer meta-analysis core (4,055 genes) is strictly DRG-derived: its six contrasts come from five studies (GSE267799, GSE212311, GSE278227, GSE241361 DRG, GSE265957 DRG) and no spinal-cord transcriptome entered that meta-analysis. The dual-ML hub set additionally pooled GSE241361 mouse spinal cord into the 72-sample training set alongside the four DRG datasets (GSE278227, GSE267799, GSE241361 DRG, GSE212311), so the 35 hubs reflect DRG-plus-spinal-cord expression convergence rather than a DRG-only signal; the remaining spinal datasets (GSE328175, GSE246288, GSE325938) were used for hub localisation only. We therefore describe the axis as a DRG-centred nerve-injury response with spinal-cord localisation, and we do not claim the hubs are DRG-exclusive.

### Human miRNA layer
GSE158825 (n=60 human plasma miRNA; lumbar surgery + pain outcome %nprs20delta) was tested by Welch+BH and Spearman+BH. miRDB v6.0 predicted hub-mRNA-targeting miRNAs; 3,511 predicted hub→miRNA relationships spanning 33 of 35 hubs were obtained, of which 752 were high-confidence (score ≥ 80) and 328 of those pairs involved human-plasma-detectable miRNAs (253 distinct miRNAs, enumerated in `results/tables/P4_hub_miRNA_human_integration.csv`). Set-level association was tested by 5,000 same-size permutation of miRNAs (p = 0.51). GSE222979 (n=1,242, three body fluids) provided cross-fluid detectability only (no pain phenotype). GSE306403 (SH-SY5Y) was retained as an in-vitro opioid-mechanism reference, not human tissue. This layer is a blood-based proxy and is interpreted only as such.

### Single-cell & spatial localisation
GSE216039 (mouse DRG neuron-enriched scRNA), GSE328175 (mouse lumbar spinal snRNA), and GSE246288 (mouse spinal-cord Cd11b+ microglia scRNA) were analysed with sample-level pseudobulk only (cell-level statistics were descriptive, never inferential, to avoid pseudoreplication). Neuron-subtype annotation used eight non-hub markers (GAL, GAP43, SOX11, VGF, MMP16, CDK5R1, SCG2, NCAM1) with zero overlap with the 35 hubs, printed for audit. Ambient-RNA index flagged unreliable cell assignments. Visium GSE325938 (mouse spinal cord) regionalised the 35 hubs on Sham/baseline tissue and cross-modally corroborated snRNA lineage calls. Regional specificity was subject to a detection floor: hubs whose detection fraction in their top region was below 5% (CDHR5, SERPINE1, VIP, REG3B, ANKRD1, plus the all-zero CRISP3 and LNP1) were flagged below-floor and excluded from biological interpretation of regional enrichment, since specificity ratios computed on near-zero detection fractions are noise-driven; per-hub floor status is marked in Supplementary Table S2. Because only Sham tissue was available, dorsal-horn assignment is reported as constitutive baseline anatomy, not injury-induced recruitment.

### Structure-based repurposing (prospectively specified)
The ligand library was ChEMBL max_phase=4 (approved-drug equivalent) → 3,085 pdbqt files (93.2% of 3,311 small molecules). **Parameter-fidelity validation was performed before screening**: exhaustiveness-1 (not max_evals) was chosen because max_evals systematically penalised flexible ligands (Spearman ρ(Δ, rotatable bonds) ≈ +0.26, top-10% recall 0%). Exhaustiveness-1 retained a rank-fidelity ceiling of ρ ≈ 0.78. Ten of 43 candidate targets had dockable holo structures with a ligand-anchored binding site suitable for box definition (GALNS, SLC2A1, TNIK, ITPKC, AXL, SERPINE1, MAPK14, VASH2, ACVR1, ADRA2A); ion-channel candidates (SCN9A/10A/11A/KCNQ2/CACNA2D1/GABRA1) have experimental structures but lack a ligand-anchored pocket appropriate for a defined docking box, and were therefore not docked (they could be revisited by blind/cavity-aware docking). Hub→target eligibility was governed by the explicit rule stated in Results (n_holo_PDB ≥ 1), which selected 17 hubs of which 9 were docked; ADRA2A was added as a non-hub external analgesic reference and TFE3, although eligible, had no definable ligand-anchored cavity. 30,850 dockings (3,085 × 10) were scored in AutoDock Vina 1.2.5. **Reverse positive controls** used ChEMBL binding-type labels (pChEMBL ≥ 6, i.e. ≤1 µM) and are reported as `control_passed` (method-validation) rather than as evidence of enrichment; **molecular-weight confounder correction** applied MW-only baseline, MW-linear-adjusted, and MW-quintile-stratified AUC. Enrichment was declared real only if docking AUC > 0.5, survived correction, and exceeded the MW-only baseline. A multivariate physicochemical control extended the single-variable MW correction: for each of the five ChEMBL-annotated targets a logistic model of known-binder status on six descriptors (MW, logP, TPSA, HBD, HBA, rotB) was fit, and a likelihood-ratio test assessed whether docking affinity added residual discrimination (Supplementary Table S4). Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the five targets was applied to both the raw full-library enrichment p-values and the size-independent (MW-adjusted) p-values (Supplementary Table S4): 4/5 raw p-values survive BH, whereas only ADRA2A's weak size-independent p survives BH, and its full-library AUC remains 0.532 (p = 0.118, NS), so a covariate-adjusted increment above the MW baseline is not interpreted as enrichment when the full-library AUC is itself indistinguishable from chance. For AXL, where a breadth-table PASS (0.880) coexists with a size-independent p of 0.141 whose confidence interval contains zero, the size-independent test is declared authoritative and the breadth-table value is reported as a method-validation control only; the two numbers answer different questions and are not both treated as enrichment verdicts.

### Multiple-testing registry
Correction was applied within, not across, analytical families, and the families are reported separately so that no single claim rests on an uncorrected test: (i) per-dataset differential expression, BH across genes; (ii) meta-analysis, BH across genes, fixed and random effects separately; (iii) gene sets, BH across the 18 multi-member sets (added in this version); (iv) docking, BH across the five ChEMBL-annotated targets for both raw and size-independent p; (v) single-cell, sample-level BH, with all cell-level statistics descriptive; (vi) multivariate physicochemical control, for each of the five ChEMBL-annotated targets a likelihood-ratio test assessed whether docking affinity added discrimination beyond chemotype (AXL p = 2.6e-5, TNIK p = 7.3e-5, ACVR1 p = 9.6e-4, MAPK14 p = 0.066, ADRA2A p = 0.027); this five-test family is reported as a post-hoc physicochemical control and is explicitly not counted among the corrected inferential tests, consistent with the composite-ranking exclusion above. Composite-ranking hypergeometric p-values are reported as descriptive retrieval metrics and are explicitly not counted among the corrected inferential tests.

### Statistical discipline, causal scope and AI-use disclosure
Single-cell differential expression was never inferred at the cell level (pseudoreplication); only sample-level pseudobulk Welch+BH across n = 2–3/group was reported, and all such localisation findings are labelled "directional hints". AUC-based enrichment p-values are one-sided; permutation-calibration p-values are two-sided, per the Statistical discipline and Multiple-testing registry paragraphs above. **Causal scope:** this is an observational reanalysis of public transcriptomes with no intervention, no temporal ordering within tissue, and no perturbation; all findings are associations, and terms such as "response" or "programme" are used descriptively rather than mechanistically. Accordingly, no causal claim, including any claim that the observed neuroimmune-metabolic coordination causes, maintains, or can be targeted to modify chronic postsurgical pain, is made or supported by this design. A generative AI language model (Claude, Anthropic) was used to assist manuscript drafting and language polishing. The scientific design, all computational analyses, results and conclusions were conceived, executed and verified by the author; every AI-generated or AI-edited sentence was checked by the author against the result tables and scripts and corrected where needed, and no AI tool was used to generate, select or interpret data. No AI tool satisfies authorship criteria.
